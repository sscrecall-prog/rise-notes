2. Core Concepts — Cosmology & Origin

Cosmology

Study of the Universe.

Universe → Galaxy → Solar System

Galaxy

Massive collection of stars, gas, dust and dark matter held together by gravity. Source examples: Milky Way and Andromeda.

Big Bang Theory

  • Given by George Lemaitre in 1927 and published in 1931.
  • Source describes an explosion of concentrated matter about 13.8 billion years ago.
  • Led to formation of galaxies, stars and other heavenly bodies, according to source.

Steady State Theory

  • Given by Fred Hoyle, Hermann Bondi and Thomas Gold.
  • Universe constantly expands while average properties remain unchanged, according to source.

Origin of Solar System

  • Began 4.8 billion years ago — source.
  • Cloud of gas and dust collapsed into a spinning disk called a Nebula.

5. Comets, Asteroids & Meteors

Comets

Large icy objects orbiting Sun; made of dust, rock and frozen gases such as water, carbon dioxide, ammonia and methane.

Halley's Comet

Seen every 76 years; source says last seen 1986 and expected in 2061.

Asteroids

Rocky, metallic or icy objects orbiting Sun.

Quadrantids

Source says this meteor shower is visible in the early month of January.

TermSource meaning
MeteoroidSmaller than asteroids; rocky or metallic space rock orbiting Sun.
MeteorMeteoroid enters Earth's mesosphere, burns up and creates a streak of light.
MeteoriteMeteoroid that hits the ground.

6. The Sun

Composition: Hydrogen 73%, Helium 25–28% and other gases.
Distance: Approx. 15 crore km / 149.6 million km from Earth.
Light: 8 min 20 sec to Earth.
Temperature: Surface 5800 K / 5600°C; centre 15.7 million K.

Layers

LayerSource description
PhotosphereOnly visible surface.
ChromosphereDim red ring above photosphere.
CoronaOutermost, extremely hot; visible during solar eclipses.

7. Planets

TerrestrialJovian
Small, rocky, solid surface, nearer Sun, few/no moons, no rings in source. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars.Large, gaseous, no solid surface, farther Sun, multiple moons, ring systems. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

Mercury

  • Closest to Sun.
  • Smallest; diameter 4900 km.
  • 88-day revolution.
  • Source: only planet with no atmosphere.

Venus

  • Hottest planet in source.
  • Earth's Twin.
  • Brightest planet in source.
  • 243-day rotation.
  • Sulphuric-acid clouds.

Earth

  • Only life-supporting planet in source.
  • Blue Planet; source says 70% water.
  • Natural satellite: Moon.
  • Density 5.5 g/cm³.
  • Closest planet to Earth: Venus — source.

Mars

  • Red Planet; iron-rich red soil.
  • Second smallest in source.
  • Phobos and Deimos.
  • Olympus Mons 22–25 km; source calls it largest volcano/tallest mountain.
  • Day 24 h 37 min.
  • Asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
  • Bennu studied by NASA OSIRIS-REx — source.

Jupiter

  • Largest planet.
  • Rotation 9 h 55 min.
  • 95 moons stated in source; Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto listed.
  • Ganymede largest among them.
  • Unclear ring; Great Red Spot.

Saturn

  • Second largest.
  • Bright concentric rings of rocks/ice.
  • Density 0.69 g/cm³ in source.
  • 274 moons stated; Titan largest.
  • Titan and Enceladus show possibility of life — source.
  • Huygens 1655; Cassini gap 1665; rotation 11 h.

Uranus

  • Green Planet due to methane — source.
  • William Herschel, 1781.
  • Hydrogen, helium, ammonia, water — source.
  • At least 27 moons; Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel.
  • 98° tilt; Rolling Planet.
  • Rotation 17 h.

Neptune

  • 165-year revolution.
  • 16-hour rotation.
  • Windiest planet — source.
  • Hydrogen + helium; blue due to methane.
  • Galle & Le Verrier, 1846.
  • Source: only planet found by mathematical prediction.
  • Triton among 14 satellites stated.

8. Moon

Earth's only natural satellite; non-luminous.
Radius 1.74 × 106 m.
Moonlight takes 1.26 seconds to reach Earth — source.
Earth–Moon distance: 3,84,000 km.
Gravity: 1/6 of Earth's.
One side visible because rotation and revolution times are same — source.
Highest point: Mt. Leibniz, identified as Mons Huygens in the source.

9. Dwarf Planets & Kuiper Belt

Source criteria

  1. Orbits Sun.
  2. Nearly spherical.
  3. Cannot clear orbit of debris.

Kuiper Belt

Ring of bodies beyond Neptune containing asteroids, rocks and comets — source.

Pluto

  • Omitted from planet list in 2006, according to source.
  • Dwarf planet; member of Kuiper Belt in source.
  • Charon largest moon; 5 moons stated.
  • Revolution 248 Earth years.
  • Oblong orbit overlaps Neptune's — source.
  • Largest and brightest dwarf planet — source.

14. Common Confusions

Meteoroid vs Meteor vs Meteorite

Space rock → atmospheric entry/streak → reaches ground.

Terrestrial vs Jovian

Terrestrial = rocky/smaller. Jovian = large/gaseous.

Photosphere vs Chromosphere vs Corona

Visible surface → dim red ring → outermost layer.

Asteroid Belt vs Kuiper Belt

Source: Asteroid Belt between Mars and Jupiter; Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune.
