1. Continental Drift Theory

According to page 2, Alfred Wegener gave Continental Drift Theory in 1912. The source also states that the idea was first proposed by Abraham Ortelius in 1596.

Pangea: The source says modern-day continents had previously been joined into a supercontinent called Pangea. The surrounding water body was called Panthalassa.

2. Mantle Convection & Sources of Heat

Page 3 states that heat driving convection cells in Earth's mantle and plate motion comes mainly from three sources:

1. Radioactive decay
Decay of elements.
2. Residual heat
Heat remaining from Earth's formation when gravitational energy was converted into heat.
3. Latent heat
Released by solidification of Earth's core as it cools.
Earth's heat→Convection cells→Plate motion

Ocean Facts from Page 3

  • Mariana Trench: described as the deepest part of the Pacific Ocean, 11 km in the source.
  • Challenger Deep: described as the deepest known point in the Mariana Trench.
  • Atlantic Ocean: described as the busiest ocean in the source.
  • Sargasso Sea: described as a unique, borderless sea in the Atlantic Ocean; brown Sargassum algae is noted.

3. Milankovitch Theory & Seafloor Spreading

Milankovitch Theory

Page 4 says it describes the collective effects of changes in Earth's movement on its climate over thousands of years.

The page again states that Abraham Ortelius's 1596 work “Thesaurus Geographicus” first suggested Earth's continents were not always in their current locations.

Seafloor Spreading

In 1962, Harry H. Hess gave Seafloor Spreading Theory. The source describes it as the process by which new oceanic crust forms at mid-ocean due to a divergent plate boundary. Magma erupts from beneath and pushes older crust away from the central ridge.

Age: Oceanic crust is younger than continental crust.
Distance: Oceanic-rock age increases as you move away from the mid-ocean ridge.
Symmetry: Rocks equidistant from the mid-ocean ridge are of the same age.
Composition: The source states oceanic crust and mantle have the same composition, “Si and Ma”.
Source wording preserved: “Si and Ma” is retained because the task is source-based; it has not been silently replaced with outside terminology.

5. Corals & Coral Bleaching

Corals

  • Marine organisms described as the “Rainforest of Sea”.
  • Live in a symbiotic relationship with Zooxanthella algae.
  • Corals secrete CaCO₃, providing protection to the algae; the algae make food for the corals.
  • Corals exist in colonies.

Favourable Conditions

ConditionRequirement stated in source
Saline waterCannot survive in fresh water.
SunlightRequired.
Clear waterRequired.
TemperatureModerate; 20°–32°C.

Coral Bleaching

When water is too warm, corals expel Zooxanthella algae living in their tissues, causing the coral to turn completely white. Page 6 identifies climate change as a major key factor in this change.

Facts from Page 6

Petroleum: Source says petroleum is extracted from the continental shelf.
Siberian Shelf: Source identifies it as the widest continental shelf, 1500 km wide.
West Coast of South America: Source identifies it as the narrowest continental shelf.
Great Barrier Reef: Source identifies it as the largest barrier reef (coral colony) in the world.

Andaman & Nicobar

Lakshadweep

The source states that Lakshadweep is mainly formed by corals, but that there are more corals in Andaman & Nicobar than Lakshadweep.

10. Common Confusions

Wegener vs Holmes

Wegener = Continental Drift Theory (1912) and the two forces listed in the source. Holmes (1930) = convection-cell explanation for the movement.

Pangea vs Panthalassa

Pangea = supercontinent. Panthalassa = surrounding water body.

Continental Shelf vs Slope

Shelf = shallowest and ≤1°. Slope = 2–5° in the source.

Seamount vs Guyot

Guyot is specifically described as a flat-topped seamount.

Fringing Reef vs Barrier Reef vs Atoll

Page 7 diagram presents them as successive stages associated with volcanic-island subsidence: active volcano → fringing reef → barrier reef → atoll.

Andaman & Nicobar vs Lakshadweep

Source links Andaman & Nicobar with subduction and Arakan Yoma; Lakshadweep is described as mainly coral-formed.
