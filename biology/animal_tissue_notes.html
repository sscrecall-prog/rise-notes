2. Epithelial Tissue

Characteristics (Pehchaan)

  • 📍 Occurrence: Puri body mein widespread.
  • Covering ya protective tissue ki tarah kaam karta hai animal body mein.
  • Cells continuous sheet banate hain — tightly packed, koi intercellular spaces nahi.
  • Basement membrane se doosre tissues se alag hote hain.
  • Function: Body ko injury ya damage se protect karta hai.

1️⃣ Simple Epithelial

  • Single layer of cells.
  • Body cavities, ducts, aur tubes ki lining.
  • 3 types: Squamous, Cuboidal, Columnar.

2️⃣ Compound Epithelial

  • Multiple layers of cells.
  • More protection provide karta hai.
  • Example: Stratified squamous (skin).

3. Squamous Epithelial Tissue

Basic Info

📍 Occurrence: Blood vessels, lungs ke air sacs (alveoli), body cavities (peritoneum) ki lining.

Cells extremely thin aur flat hote hain — irregular shape.

Simple Squamous

  • 📍 Mouth aur oesophagus (food pipe).
  • Single layer — flattened with irregular boundaries.
  • Delicate lining banate hain.

Stratified Squamous

  • 📍 Skin mein hota hai.
  • Cells layers mein arranged hote hain.
  • Wear and tear ko prevent karta hai.

4. Cuboidal Epithelial Tissue

Basic Info

  • 📍 Occurrence: Salivary glands, sweat glands, thyroid glands, kidney tubules ki lining.
  • Cells cube-like structure mein hote hain — height aur width equal hoti hai.

5. Columnar & Ciliated Epithelial Tissue

Columnar Epithelial

  • 📍 Intestine, stomach aur gallbladder ki lining.
  • Cells pillar-like, tall aur wide hote hain.
  • Nuclei base pe located hote hain.

Ciliated Epithelial

  • 📍 Respiratory tract, urinary tubules, kidney.
  • Cells cuboidal/columnar — free surface pe hair-like projections = Cilia.
  • Function: Mucus, urine ka movement cilia ki rhythmic beating se.

7. Skeletal Muscle

Key Facts

  • 📍 Occurrence: Skeletal part of body (bones wala part).
  • Fibres elongated, unbranched aur cylindrical.
  • Fast in nature.
  • Voluntary muscles — hamare control mein hain.
  • Each cell multinucleated hota hai — striations (dark aur light bands) hoti hain.
  • Easily fatigue ho jaate hain.
  • Function: Body movement aur posture maintain karna.
↑ Back to Topics

8. Smooth Muscle

Key Facts

  • 📍 Occurrence: Intestine, uterus, blood vessels, stomach, bronchi, iris.
  • Nucleus cell ke centre mein hota hai.
  • Cells non-striated, spindle-shaped (fusiform) — cell junction se held together.
  • Unbranched, slow in growth.
  • Easily fatigue nahi hote.
  • Involuntary muscles — hamare control mein NAHI.
  • Function: Peristalsis — wave-like movement jo food aur substances ko digestive tract mein propel karta hai.
↑ Back to Topics

9. Cardiac Muscle

Key Facts

  • 📍 Occurrence: Heart.
  • Fibres long, cylindrical.
  • Each fibre uninucleated aur branched hota hai.
  • Striations present hoti hain.
  • Cardiac muscles fatigue nahi hote — lifelong kaam karte hain!
  • Involuntary muscles — hamare control mein NAHI.
  • Blood pump aur distribute karne mein help karte hain.
  • Function: Heart ki wall mein contraction aur relaxation.

11. Connective Tissue

Kya hota hai?

  • 📍 Occurrence: Tendons, cartilage, adipose tissue, ligaments, dermis.
  • Alag-alag body parts ko connect karta hai.
  • Cells intracellular matrix mein embedded hote hain — matrix jelly (cartilage), fluid (blood), dense ya rigid (bone) ho sakta hai.
  • Binding, supporting aur packing material ki tarah kaam karta hai.
  • Organs ko body movements se displace hone se rokta hai.

12. Areolar Connective Tissue

Loose Connective Tissue

  • 📍 Skin aur muscles ke beech, organs ke andar spaces mein, bone marrow mein.
  • Loose, gel-like matrix — cells, fibers aur ground substance se bana.
  • Support, protection aur cushioning deta hai various body structures ko.
  • Loosely arranged network of fibers — semi-fluid matrix mein → flexible aur adaptable.

Dense Connective Tissue

High proportion of fibers — mainly Collagen. 2 types:

Tendons

  • Fibrous tissue — great strength, limited flexibility.
  • Muscle → Bone se connect karta hai.

Ligaments

  • Considerable strength, small amount of matrix.
  • Bone → Bone se connect karta hai.
Trick: T for Tendon = T for "To" muscle-to-bone | L for Ligament = L for "Link" bone-to-bone

13. Adipose Connective Tissue

Key Facts

  • 📍 Occurrence: Skin ke neeche (jyada body fat → zyada adipose tissue), internal organs ke beech.
  • Cells round/oval shaped — fat cells (adipocytes) ka aggregation.

    • 🛡️ Mechanical shock se body ko bachata hai.

  • Example: Camel ka hump (fat store karta hai).

20. Anatomy of a Neuron

Neuron = Nervous system ki basic structural aur fundamental unit | Nerve impulses transmit karne ke liye specialized | Human body ka sabse lamba cell!
📌 Discovery

Camillo Golgi ne neuron ki structure discover ki — "Black Reaction" (Golgi's method) use karke.

Cell Body (Soma)
Nucleus aur cytoplasm contain karta hai — neuron ki activities control karta hai.
Dendrites
Cell body se short, branched extensions — doosre neurons ya sensory cells se signals receive karte hain. Impulses cell body ki taraf carry karte hain.
Axon
Nerve impulses ko cell body se door — doosre neurons ya effectors (muscles/glands) tak carry karta hai.
Myelin Sheath
Fatty layer jo axon ko cover karta hai — axon ko insulate karta hai aur signal transmission speed up karta hai.
Schwann Cells
Glial cells jo brain ke bahar axons pe Myelin Sheath banate hain.
Axon Terminals (Synaptic knobs)
Axon ke end branches — neurotransmitters contain karte hain. Synapse ke through signals next neuron ya effector cell tak transmit karte hain.

21. Transmission of Neuron Impulses

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Step 1: Stimulus dendrites receive karte hain.
  2. Step 2: Electrical impulse cell body aur axon se travel karta hai.
  3. Step 3: Axon terminals pe impulse neurotransmitters release trigger karta hai.
  4. Step 4: Neurotransmitters synapse cross karte hain → signal next neuron ya effector (muscle/gland) tak pass hota hai.
  5. Step 5: Response hoti hai!
Flow: Stimulus → Dendrites → Cell Body → Axon → Axon Terminal → Synapse → Neurotransmitters → Next Neuron/Effector → Response
