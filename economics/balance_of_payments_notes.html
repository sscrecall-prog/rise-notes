Balance of Payments (BoP) – Introduction

BoP Kya Hota Hai?

Definition: Balance of Payments ek systematic record hai jisme ek country ke saare financial transactions — imports, exports, investments — baaki duniya ke saath ek specific period (usually 1 year) me record hote hain.

🏠 Real Life Example

Ghar ka hisaab kitaab sochlo: Tumhare ghar me jo paise aate hain (salary, rent income) aur jo bahar jaate hain (groceries, bills, EMI) — sab ka record ek register me hota hai. Bilkul waise hi, ek country ka BoP us country ka "international hisaab kitaab" hai.

Nature: BoP ek desh ki balance sheet ki tarah kaam karta hai for external trade.
Structure: BoP mainly 2 parts me divide hota hai — 1. Current Account & 2. Capital Account
2

Current Account

Current Account me goods aur services ki incoming aur outgoing track hoti hai countries ke beech. Yeh actual trade reflect karta hai.

💡 Yaad Rakhne ka Trick

Current = Abhi jo chal raha hai, daily ka trade. Jaise tumhara "current" bank account daily transactions ke liye hota hai!

Current Account ke 3 Parts:

① Visible Items (Trade in Goods)

Tangible cheezein — jo deikh sako, chhoo sako — unka import/export.

📦 Examples

Tea, Coffee, Machinery, Mobile phones, Cars — ye sab visible items hain. Jab India basmati rice America bhejta hai, wo visible export hai. Jab India petrol Saudi Arabia se khareedata hai, wo visible import hai.

② Invisible Items (Trade in Services)

Intangible cheezein — jo deikh nahi sakte — unka import/export. Services hoti hain ye.

💻 Examples

IT Services, Shipping, Banking, Tele-calling: Jab Infosys ya TCS American company ke liye software banata hai, to India invisible exports kar raha hai. Jab Indian students America jaate hain padhne, to India education services import kar raha hai.

③ Transfer Payments (Unilateral Transfers)

One-way transfers — koi cheez receive nahi hoti return me. Components: Remittances, Foreign Aid, Grants.

💸 Real Life Example

Remittances: Raju Dubai me kaam karta hai aur har mahine ghar ₹50,000 bhejta hai. Yahi hai remittance! India is currently World's #1 recipient of remittances. Mexico is #2.

3

Capital Account

Capital Account me assets ki buying & selling track hoti hai — jaise land, property, stocks, bonds — between countries.

🏦 Yaad Rakhne ka Trick

Capital = Badi cheezein, assets. Jaise tumhara "capital" (principal amount) long-term kaam aata hai. Capital account = long-term investments ka hisaab.

① FDI – Foreign Direct Investment
  • Long-term investment in physical assets of a foreign country.
  • Involves ownership ya management control lena.
  • Stable investment maana jaata hai — factory nahi bech sakte jaldi se.
🛒 Classic Example

Walmart ne Flipkart acquire kiya — yeh FDI ka perfect example hai! Walmart (American company) ne India ki Flipkart me $16 billion invest kiya aur ownership le li. Foreign company, Indian business me direct investment.

② FPI / FII – Foreign Portfolio Investment
  • Short-term investment in financial assets (shares, bonds).
  • No management control — sirf paisa lagao, profit lo.
  • Examples: Stocks, Bonds, Mutual Funds, NRI Deposits
🔥 "Hot Money" kya hota hai?

FPI ko "Hot Money" kehte hain kyunki ye bahut volatile hai. Jab market unstable hoti hai, foreign investors apne stocks/bonds turant bech ke country chhod dete hain. Real example: 2008 financial crisis ke time, FIIs ne Indian stock market se record speed me paisa nikala — Sensex crash ho gaya!

③ FDI Routes & Sectoral Limits
  • Automatic Route: Government approval ki zaroorat nahi. Seedha invest karo.
  • Government Route: Pehle government se permission leni padti hai.
  • Defence Sector: 74% tak = Automatic Route. 74%–100% = Government Route.
  • Insurance Sector: 100% FDI allowed via Automatic Route.
🏭 Simple Example

Agar koi foreign company India me cement factory lagana chahti hai → Automatic Route (direct aa sakte hain). Agar koi defence equipment factory lagani hai 80% ownership ke saath → Government Route (permission leni padegi).

🏗️ FDI (Foreign Direct Investment)

  • Long-term investment
  • Physical assets (factories, land)
  • Management control hota hai
  • Stable, "Cool Money"
  • Good for economy

📈 FPI (Foreign Portfolio Inv.)

  • Short-term investment
  • Financial assets (stocks)
  • No management control
  • Volatile, "Hot Money"
  • Can destabilize market

Deficits & 1991 Crisis

Current Account Deficit (CAD)

Definition: Jab ek country import zyada kare aur export kam, tab CAD hota hai. Matlab: Imports > Exports

🏠 Ghar ka Example

Maan lo Rahul ki salary ₹30,000 hai lekin woh ₹40,000 kharcha karta hai. Matlab wo ₹10,000 deficit me hai. Bilkul waise hi, agar India ₹5 lakh crore export kare par ₹7 lakh crore import kare, toh ₹2 lakh crore ka CAD hai.


  • Key Insight: CAD = country net borrower hai duniya se.
  • India's Current CAD: ~1.2% of GDP (RBI comfortable level = 2–2.5%).
  • Surplus Era (2000–2003): IT/BPO boom aur kam imports ki wajah se India surplus me tha.
Twin Deficit 👫

Jab dono deficits ek saath ho jaate hain:

Fiscal Deficit

Government ka kharcha > Government ki income
Formula: Total Expenditure – Total Receipts (excl. borrowings)

Current Account Deficit

Import Bill > Export Earnings
Country zyada bahar se khareed rahi hai

⚠️ Real Life Twin Deficit

Imagine karo Ramesh ka business loss me hai (fiscal deficit) AUR woh ghar ke liye bahar se zyada khareedne lag gaya (CAD). Dono problem ek saath = Twin Deficit = Bahut buri situation!

🚨 1991 Balance of Payments Crisis

Also called: Currency Crisis

  • 1990-91 tak India ka foreign exchange reserve almost khatam ho gaya tha.
  • India ke paas sirf 2 hafton (fortnight) ka import cover tha.
  • Country na imports pay kar sakti thi, na foreign debts service kar sakti thi.
😰 Imagine Karo

Imagine karo ghar me sirf 14 din ka rashan bacha hai aur pocket khali hai. Na credit card, na loan. Iss haalat me tum kya karoge? Kisi se madad maangoge. Bilkul yahi India ne bhi kiya!


The Problem
  • Forex reserves depleted
  • Only 2 weeks import cover
  • Couldn't pay foreign debt
The Solution
  • IMF se bailout loan maanga
  • Sone ko mortgage kiya (UK me)
  • Structural reforms ki shuruaat

🏛️ Key Architects of 1991 Reform:
PM: P.V. Narasimha Rao  |  Finance Minister: Dr. Manmohan Singh

LPG Reforms (1991) – New Economic Policy

LPG = Liberalisation + Privatisation + Globalisation
1991 crisis se bahar nikalne ke liye "New Economic Policy" launch ki gayi jisme ye 3 reforms the.
L – Liberalisation (Udarikaran) 🔓

Definition: Government ki unnecessary restrictions hatana economy se.

  • Abolition of License Raj: Zyaadatar industries ko license ki zaroorat khatam.
  • Tariff Reduction: Import duties kam ki — foreign goods aane lage.
  • Markets deregulate kiye — competition badhaya.
🏭 Real Life Example

1991 se pehle: Agar Tata Motors car factory lagana chahti, to Delhi me government offices ke 20 chakkar laganke lagta tha, mahine ka wait. After liberalisation: Directly shuru karo. License Raj khatam! Maruti se competition badhao, quality improve karo!

P – Privatisation (Nijikaran) 🏢

Definition: Government enterprises ka ownership/management private sector ko transfer karna.

  • Objective: Efficiency badhana aur government ka financial burden kam karna.
✈️ Real Life Example

Air India government ki thi, losses me thi. Tata Group ne 2022 me khareed li. Ab better service, better management. Yahi hai privatisation! Similarly, PSU disinvestment bhi privatisation ka hi part hai.

G – Globalisation (Vaishvikaran) 🌍

Definition: National economy ko global economy se integrate karna — goods, services, capital, technology, labor ka free flow badhana.

📱 Real Life Example

Globalisation ki wajah se aaj Apple, Samsung, McDonald's, Amazon — sab India me hain. Aur Infosys, TCS, Wipro America aur Europe me kaam kar rahi hain. Dunia ek badi market ban gayi!

Forex Reserves (Videshi Mudra Bhandar)

Definition: Forex reserves wo assets hain jo RBI (India ki central bank) rakhti hai.

Purpose: Import cover provide karna aur external shocks (recessions, wars, currency volatility) se economy ko protect karna.

🏦 Real Life Comparison

Ghar me emergency fund: Usi tarah jaise tum apne ghar me 6 mahine ka emergency fund rakhte ho — RBI bhi India ke liye foreign currencies reserve me rakhti hai. 1991 me sirf 2 hafte ka tha, aaj almost 1 saal ka hai!

IMF & World Bank

History – Bretton Woods Conference 1944
  • Year: 1944 (officially called: United Nations Monetary & Financial Conference)
  • Participants: 44 nations, 730 delegates
  • Result: IMF + World Bank (IBRD) — "Twin Organisations" bane
  • Why: 1929-30 Great Depression ke baad world ne socha — ek global financial system chahiye
  • HQ: Washington D.C. (dono ka)
🌍 Kya Karta Hai IMF?

IMF ek "global doctor" ki tarah hai financially sick countries ke liye. 1991 me India beemar tha (broke tha), IMF ne loan diya — lekin conditions ke saath (reforms karo). Argentina, Pakistan, Sri Lanka — sab IMF ke paas jaate hain jab broke hote hain.

Nostro & Vostro Accounts

NOSTRO (Our)

"Our account in YOUR bank"

Domestic bank ka account foreign bank me, foreign currency me.

💡 Example

SBI ka account Citibank (USA) me — dollars me. Jab India ko USA se payment receive karni ho, SBI us Nostro account use karta hai.

VOSTRO (Your)

"YOUR account in OUR bank"

Foreign bank ka account domestic bank me, domestic currency me.

💡 Example

Citibank (USA) ka account HDFC Bank me — rupees me. Jab US bank India me transactions karna chahti, wo HDFC ke paas apna Vostro account use karti hai.

🧠 Memory Trick: NOSTRO = "hamara account unke yahan" (No-Stro = No Stranger = apna account). VOSTRO = "unka account hamare yahan" (Vo = Woh = wo log).

Different Types of Currencies

1. Hard Currency 💪

High liquidity + universal acceptability. Internationally widely trusted.

Example: US Dollar (USD)

🌍 Why "Hard"?

Kisi bhi country me jao, dollar accept hoga. Dubai, Singapore, Paris — har jagah. Isliye ye "hard" hai — reliable, stable, widely accepted. India me bhi dollar se bahut cheezein hoti hain (oil, gold ka hisaab dollar me).

2. Soft Currency 😔

Easily available, lower liquidity, lower acceptability. Developing nations ki currencies.

Example: Indian Rupee (INR)

💔 Why "Soft"?

Agar tum Singapore jao aur rupee nikalo, to wo log nahi lenge. Dollars/Euros chahiye. INR sirf India me chal ta hai essentially. Isliye rupee "soft currency" hai.

3. Hot Currency 🔥

Wo currency jo bahut quickly ek economy me aati aur bahar jaati hai.

🌡️ Example

Jab countries de-dollarize hoti hain (dollar pe dependency kam karti hain) — dollar rapidly un economies se bahar jaata hai. Isliye dollar "hot" ban jaata hai un contexts me.

4. Heated Currency 🌡️

Domestic currency under high pressure of depreciation — value rapidly girna.

🇵🇰 Example

Pakistani Rupee ek "heated currency" ka example hai — dollar ke against rapidly depreciate hoti rahi (1 USD = 300+ PKR). Economy unstable, currency "heated" ho gayi.

5. Cheap Currency 📉

Government expansionary policy follow kare → bonds wapas khareedta hai → liquidity inject → currency ka supply badh jaata hai → value gir jaati hai → "Cheap".

💰 Real Life

Zyada paisa print karo → zyada supply → value gire = Cheap. Like Zimbabwe ne bahut paisa print kiya — currency almost worthless ho gayi!

6. Dear Currency 📈

Government contractionary policy follow kare → bonds sell karta hai → liquidity soaks up → currency supply ghatne lagti hai → value badhti hai → "Dear" (expensive).

💰 Real Life

RBI bonds bechta hai → market se paisa kheench leta hai → rupee ki supply kam → rupee "dear" (strong) ho jaata hai.

Currency Valuation – 4 Concepts

Currency ki value 2 tarike se change hoti hai: Market Forces (floating) ya Government Intervention (fixed).

🌊 Market-Driven (Floating Exchange Rate):

📉 Depreciation

Market forces se domestic currency ki value gir jaati hai.

Logic: Supply high, Demand low → value falls

Example: $1 = ₹80 → $1 = ₹90

Exporters benefit ✅

🚀 Real Example

Infosys ko USA se $1 million milte hain. Pehle = ₹8 Cr. After depreciation = ₹9 Cr. Exporters ko faida!

📈 Appreciation

Market forces se domestic currency ki value badh jaati hai.

Logic: Demand high → value rises

Example: $1 = ₹90 → $1 = ₹80

Importers benefit ✅

🛒 Real Example

iPhone $1000 ka → Pehle = ₹90,000. After appreciation = ₹80,000. Importers ko faida!

🏛️ Government-Driven (Fixed Exchange Rate):

📉 Devaluation

Government deliberately apni currency ki value kam karti hai.

Purpose: Exports boost karna (foreign buyers ke liye sasta).

🌟 India Example

India ne 1966 aur 1991 me rupee devalue kiya tha — taaki exports competitive ho sakein. Government ka deliberate decision tha.

📈 Revaluation

Government deliberately apni currency ki value badhati hai (fixed rate system me).

Effect: Imports saste hote hain.

🇨🇳 Real Example

China pe US pressure dalta hai yuan revalue karne ke liye taaki Chinese exports US ke liye mahenge ho jayen. China kabhi kabhi karta hai yeh.

Concept Cause Effect on Exports Benefit to
Depreciation 📉 Market (Supply > Demand) ✅ Positive (Competitive) Exporters
Appreciation 📈 Market (Demand > Supply) ❌ Negative Importers
Devaluation 📉 Government/Central Bank ✅ Positive (Boost) Exporters
Revaluation 📈 Government/Central Bank ❌ Negative Importers

Exchange Rate Systems & Indices

India ka Exchange Rate – Timeline
1928 – 1948
Indian Rupee British Pound Sterling se pegged tha (fixed).
Post 1948 (IMF Era)
IMF ke baad India ne rupee ko US Dollar se peg kiya. Fixed Exchange Rate System (e.g., $1 = ₹20 fixed).
1975
India ne rupee ko Pound Sterling se completely delink kiya.
March 1992 – LERMS
Liberalised Exchange Rate Management System introduce hua. Dual system — kuch market, kuch government determine karta tha.
1992–1993
India moved to Floating Exchange Rate System — market forces determine karti hain rupee ki value.
NEER – Nominal Effective Exchange Rate

Weighted average of bilateral exchange rates against a basket of currencies.

  • Weights = trade importance of each country
  • NEER increase = Rupee appreciation
  • Inflation ke liye adjust nahi karta
REER – Real Effective Exchange Rate

NEER adjusted for inflation differences.

  • Formula: REER = NEER − Inflation Differential
  • REER zyada realistic picture deta hai
  • Inflation consider karta hai ✅
📊 NEER vs REER – Simple Example

NEER: Dollar ke against rupee 5% appreciate hua — NEER bata dega.
REER: But agar India me inflation 6% thi aur USA me 1% — to actually rupee REAL me 2% depreciate hua. REER ye real picture deta hai!

FERA vs FEMA & Rupee Convertibility

⛓️ FERA (1973)

Foreign Exchange Regulation Act

  • Intent: Forex resources conserve karna
  • Draconian (bahut strict) law
  • Violation = Criminal offense
  • Reason for repeal: Economic growth ro raha tha
😰 FERA Kaisa Tha?

Agar galti se foreign currency exchange ki bina permission ke — seedha jail! Bahut strict tha. Business ke liye nightmare.

✅ FEMA (1999)

Foreign Exchange Management Act

  • Post-1991 reforms me FERA replace kiya
  • Business-friendly law
  • Violation = Civil offense (criminal nahi)
  • Focus: Forex enhance karna, trade promote karna
😊 FEMA Kaisa Hai?

Abhi agar koi forex niyam tode — fine lagega, jail nahi. Much more business-friendly. Liberalisation ka part!

Convertibility of Rupee 🔄

Definition: Ease with which rupee ko gold ya kisi aur currency me convert kar sako.

① Current Account Convertibility
  • Goods & services ke trade ke liye currency convert karo.
  • India: Full Convertibility ✅
  • Achieved: August 1994
  • IMF Article VIII se compliant
✈️ Example

Aaj tum foreign trip ke liye rupees ko dollars me freely convert kar sakte ho. Yahi current account convertibility hai!

② Capital Account Convertibility
  • Capital transactions ke liye — foreign assets, real estate buy karna.
  • India: Partial (40:60 ratio)
  • Abhi fully convertible nahi
🏠 Example

Agar tum USA me property kharidna chahte ho — kuch restrictions hain. Partial convertibility hai — poori freedom nahi. Isliye Tarapore Committee bani!

🏛️ Tarapore Committees (1997 & 2006): RBI ne S.S. Tarapore ke under committees banaayi — Full Capital Account Convertibility ka roadmap taiyar karne ke liye. Pre-conditions: Low fiscal deficit + Low inflation + Low NPAs. Abhi tak fully implement nahi hua.
